# Style Variant Matrix

Use this matrix to describe a style without changing the underlying story or identity.

| Variant | Medium | Palette and light | Composition | Typography policy | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `modern-anime-film` | polished anime background art, restrained linework | blue-violet shadows, coral sunset, cinematic rim light | full-bleed vertical scenes with title negative space | typeset outside the image | complete narrative pages |
| `retro-scrapbook` | paper collage, polaroids, tape, hand-drawn marks | warm paper, faded coral, muted ink | layered photos and illustrated memories | editable title overlay | cover and anniversary keepsake |
| `semi-realistic-painted` | realistic faces with soft painterly treatment | film neutrals and warm highlights | portrait or intimate environmental scenes | small editorial captions | identity-forward portraits |
| `watercolor-memory` | loose watercolor wash and pencil texture | cream, dusty blue, rose, low contrast | airy pages with large quiet areas | handwritten-style overlay after visual QA | gentle nostalgic pages |
| `editorial-photo-diary` | real photos plus restrained graphic frames | neutral paper with one accent color | photo sequence, contact sheets, captions | strong grid and small labels | print presentation |

For a new variant, specify:

1. Name and intended output format.
2. Invariants: story facts, character references, clothing continuity, page IDs, and consent.
3. Changes: medium, palette, lighting, camera language, texture, layout, and typography.
4. One identity test and one representative scene for acceptance.
5. Negative constraints: no extra people, brand marks, generated text, identity blending, malformed hands, or invented events.
